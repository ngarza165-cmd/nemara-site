01Why we exist

Nature has become a risk institutions must manage. Their tools were built to count it.

Bears are entering towns in Japan in record numbers. The largest companies in Europe must now account for their impact on biodiversity. In both cases someone is accountable for a decision — where to send a response team, what to tell a regulator — and the evidence they need is scattered across sensors, surveys, field reports and spreadsheets.

Today's environmental tools are good at telling you what happened. Very few tell you what is likely to happen next, how serious it is, or what to do about it — with reasons that still stand up afterwards. That is the gap Nemara exists to close.

i.

Nature is now a mandate

Governments carry public-safety duties for wildlife and natural hazards. Regulators require companies to disclose their impact on nature. The urgency is written into law, and funded.

ii.

The signals already exist

Satellite imagery, sensor networks, field surveys, incident records. What is missing is not more data — it is the layer that connects it and reads it forward.

iii.

Decisions must be defensible

When a response is sent to a predicted location, or a disclosure is audited, the reasoning has to be shown, not asserted. Black boxes do not survive scrutiny.

03How it works

From what is, to what to do.

Four layers, each built on the one before. The intelligence lives in the structure, not in any single model — so it improves as data arrives, without changing what decision-makers see.

1
What is

Ontology

Every animal, place, habitat, incident and asset becomes a linked entity that can be reasoned over.

2
What will be

Prediction

Movement and documented behaviour — seasonal food scarcity, dawn and dusk activity — projected forward.

3
How serious

Risk

A weighted score for every entity, decomposed into the named factors that drive it.

4
What to do

Decision

A recommended action and a plain-language reason a duty officer can act on — and defend.

Explainable by design

Every score shows its working.

Our method comes from financial-crime compliance, where every alert must justify itself to an auditor. A Nemara risk score is never a single unexplained number: it breaks down into the factors behind it, each one traceable to its evidence.

04What we believe

Trust is the product.

a.

Anticipate, don't just record

The value of a warning is the time it buys. We read the signals that arrive before harm, not the reports that follow it.

b.

Show the reasoning

A decision that cannot explain itself cannot be audited, challenged or improved. Ours always can.

c.

Be willing to be wrong in public

We publish our forecasts and correct them openly when new data arrives. A model that is never shown to be wrong is not being tested.

d.

Verify, don't sell

We do not issue environmental credits. We aim to be the independent party that checks whether an environmental outcome actually happened.

05Where we start

Two hemispheres. Two mandates. One platform.

JapanFirst focus

Human–wildlife conflict

Japan has just lived through its worst year of bear attacks on record, and the government has made it a national priority. Response teams are shrinking while encounters rise: the scarce capacity that remains has to be aimed before the encounter, not after.

MandatePublic safety

EuropeIn development

Nature data for disclosure

Under the EU's ESRS E4 standard, Europe's largest companies must assess their impact on biodiversity and, where it is material, report it. They need primary, verifiable nature data — not another reporting template.

MandateCorporate sustainability reporting

The same ontology and risk engine serve both. A new market is configuration, not a rebuild — which is how a focused company can work across very different landscapes.
